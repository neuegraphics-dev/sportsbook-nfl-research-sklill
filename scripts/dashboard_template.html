BookMaker.eu is treated as the sharp reference book: its two-sided moneyline gets "de-vigged" — the book's built-in margin stripped back out — to estimate the market's true, fair win probability for each side. Any other book paying more than that fair probability implies is +EV: a real mathematical edge over the long run, though — unlike arbitrage — not a guaranteed win on any single game.

De-vig — take BookMaker's favorite and underdog implied probabilities (1 / decimal odds, each side), then divide each by their sum so they add back up to 100%. That strips the vig out and leaves a fair probability per side.
Expected value — EV% = (book's decimal odds × BookMaker's fair probability) − 1. Positive means that book is paying more than the fair price says it should.

+EV isn't a guaranteed win. It means the price is better than fair, not that the bet wins. The edge is meant to show up on average across many independent +EV bets — on any single game, the side you're "supposed" to have the edge on can still lose.
The reference book can be wrong too. BookMaker.eu is used here because it's a real book with two-sided current prices, not because it's provably the sharpest number available — treat this as one credible fair-value estimate, not gospel.
Small edges disappear fast. Anything under roughly 1–2% is within the normal noise of vig differences between books, and can flip the next time either line moves.

Arbitrage ("arbing") means betting every outcome of one game across different books so that no matter who wins, you cash more than you staked — because the books disagree with each other by more than their combined vig. It only works while both prices hold long enough to place both legs.

Implied probability — L = 1/Odds_A + 1/Odds_B (decimal odds). If L < 1.00, a risk-free margin exists.
Stake split — Stake_A = Total × (1/Odds_A) / L, Stake_B fills the rest — both legs return the same payout, Total / L.

Before you place two legs

Match the settlement rules first. Confirm both books grade the game the same way (overtime, pushes, void conditions) before you split stakes across them — a mismatch turns a "guaranteed" arb into a one-sided loss.
Take the bigger-liability leg first. Place the underdog / longer-odds leg before the favorite leg, then immediately place the second — it's the price more likely to move first.
Round stakes to normal-looking numbers. $479.07 reads as calculator output; $480 reads like an ordinary wager. This isn't about hiding anything — oddly precise stakes are simply one of the easiest patterns for a book's risk team to flag, and a flagged account gets limited before it ever compounds.
Recreational books limit winners. Sustained arbitrage is one of the fastest ways to get stake-limited or "gubbed" — this only keeps working at books still willing to take your action.
Palpable errors get voided. A clearly mistyped line can be voided by the book after the fact, leaving your other leg unhedged.
The line can move before your second click lands. Any delay between leg one and leg two is where a real arb turns into a real loss.

A 2-team, 6-point "Wong teaser" only clears its own break-even when both legs' original spreads sit in a specific window — because the 6 points have to cross both key numbers, 3 and 7, to lift your win rate enough to cover the worse odds a teaser pays. Qualification checks the best available current spread across captured books — Caesars first (where these actually get placed), falling back to DraftKings, BookMaker.eu, or FanDuel when Caesars hasn't been captured for a game, so a book gap doesn't quietly drop a game from consideration. The total-ceiling check still borrows DraftKings' total, because Caesars doesn't expose a fetchable total from any source tested so far. Every leg and card below is labeled with which book its spread number actually came from — treat a non-Caesars flag as "worth checking," and confirm the exact price at Caesars before betting it.

~15% of NFL games land on a 3-point margin, ~8-10% on 7 points — together nearly a quarter of all outcomes. A standard spread covers close to 50% of the time; teasing 6 points only pushes that to the 73-75% a teaser needs, and only if the move crosses both numbers.
That happens in exactly two spots: a favorite of exactly -7.5 teases down to -1.5, or an underdog of +1.5 to +2.5 teases up to +7.5/+8.5. Outside those, the 6 points cross zero, one, or the wrong key number — the math doesn't clear. Points are also worth more in low-scoring games, so only bet when the total is 47 or lower, no exceptions above that.
The -8.5 fallacy: a -8.5 favorite looks like it belongs in the same window as -7.5 -- it isn't. Teasing 6 points off -8.5 lands at -2.5, not -1.5, and -2.5 still needs a 3+ point win to cash. It never actually clears the 3, so it's rejected outright rather than treated as a softer version of the -7.5 leg.

When to place it: day of the game

WagerTalk's own guidance is to bet Wong teasers on the day of the game itself, not days ahead of it -- by kickoff day, both legs' final injury reports/inactives and any weather are already known, at the cost of whatever the line does between now and then (which can go either way -- see "The trap" below for what a wrong-direction move looks like). (Corrected 2026-09-23: this section previously argued for a Tuesday–Thursday, 3–6-days-out window on this project's own line-efficiency reasoning -- that wasn't sourced from WagerTalk and didn't match their actual stated approach.)

Suggested combos to parlay

Both legs of a 2-team teaser are bet day-of-game (see above), so they can only be combined if they actually kick off on the same calendar day -- pairing a Thursday leg with a Sunday leg isn't a real ticket, since you're not placing both bets on the same day. Legs are grouped by kickoff day first, then paired lowest-combined-total-first within each day group. A Thursday or Monday game usually has no other game on its own day, so it typically can't be combo'd at all -- it still shows below as a solo-qualifying leg. Checklist before betting a teaser: (1) favorite leg is exactly -7.5 (never -8.5) or underdog leg is +1.5 to +2.5 on the best available current spread (Caesars when captured, otherwise DraftKings/BookMaker.eu/FanDuel — each card says which), (2) if the flagged number came from a book other than Caesars, confirm the exact spread at Caesars before betting — a flag from another book is enough signal to go check, not a guarantee the Caesars number matches exactly, (3) both games' totals are 47 or lower — no exceptions, sourced from DraftKings since Caesars doesn't expose a fetchable total, (4) book price is -120 or better (excellent), or -125 only if the spread is locked exactly at a qualifying number (acceptable) — anything worse than -125, skip that book for this bet, (5) placed on the day of the game -- and for a 2-team combo, both legs kicking off that same calendar day.
